RecSoup：面向可控多目标推荐的高效模型合并方法

核心 7/10
RecSoup: Efficient Model Merging Approach for Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation
Zidong Jia, Guofu Xie, Xiao Zhang
核心总结:

论文研究可控多目标推荐（CMOR），旨在推理时动态调整目标间权衡而无需重训练。核心思路是通过两阶段模型合并：先基于目标特定重要性和跨目标相关性剪枝以缓解参数干扰，再学习偏好到系数的映射实现自适应参数合并，从而在推理时灵活导航帕累托前沿。

Recommender systems often need to balance multiple conflicting objectives. Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation (CMOR) enables dynamic adjustment of objective trade-offs at inference time without retraining, adapting to shifting user or system requirements. Existing CMOR approaches typically rely on output-level reranking or parameter-generating modules, which either limit the scope of feasible trade-offs or incur substantial training and inference cost. Model merging provides a promising alternative by reusing multiple objective-specific models via parameter combination. However, its direct application to recommender systems is challenging due to the lack of a shared linear regime and cross-objective interference. To address these issues, we propose RecSoup, a lightweight two-stage approach for effective CMOR via specialized model merging. RecSoup first mitigates parameter interference across objectives through a pruning strategy that jointly considers objective-specific importance and cross-objective relevance. It then performs adaptive parameter merging via a learnable preference-to-coefficient mapping, allowing dynamic trade-off control at inference time without joint retraining or additional generative modules. Theoretically, we prove that RecSoup outperforms the parameter generation paradigm in effectiveness and efficiency, as it can fully navigate the Pareto front without smoothness constraints. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that RecSoup achieves improved Pareto front quality and effective controllability while delivers 165--228x faster inference-time latency than parameter generation approaches. Code is available at https://github.com/35798642/RECSOUP.git.

查看完整摘要
Permutation generation with language models requires both text-conditioned ranking and a global one-to-one assignment between candidates and output positions. We study this problem through three diffusion-LLM parameterizations: hard-assignment prediction over shuffled candidate-index sequences, soft-assignment prediction over doubly stochastic matrices, and direct ordering-score prediction followed by sorting. The three families place permutation constraints at different stages of prediction and decoding and therefore support different training objectives and sampling procedures. We evaluate these designs on recommendation reranking across three Amazon domains with candidate sets of size 10 and 20. At one function evaluation, the hard- and soft-assignment variants achieve Recall@1 comparable to a constrained masked-diffusion baseline, whereas direct ordering-score prediction guarantees valid permutations without Hungarian assignment or Sinkhorn normalization. Additional function evaluations do not consistently improve ranking quality, and their effect depends strongly on the state representation and training objective. These results show that corruption, prediction parameterization, objective, and decoder should be designed jointly for permutation diffusion with language-model denoisers.

We present TransX, a sequence-transduction framework and co-designed infrastructure for scaling industrial recommendation with explicit control over model-compute allocation and execution amortization. TransX separates sequence encoding, behavior memory, and candidate-conditioned fusion into independently scalable components. Training amortizes causal trajectory computation across behavioral prefixes and fans out memory states to candidates; serving disaggregates bulk prefill, nearline refresh, and online prediction through compact behavior-memory KV. We observe anisotropic scaling returns: encoder scaling may saturate beyond moderate depth at fixed memory resolution and fusion capacity; increasing supervision shifts the allocation frontier toward candidate-conditioned fusion; and the memory resolution needed to preserve quality remains roughly stable as model capacity grows. We train TransX on click and conversion, post-train its shared behavioral interface for retrieval and preference ranking, and explore generative recommendation offline. In separate production A/B experiments, the core TransX model improves CTR by 6.02% and conversion by 4.45%, while post-trained retrieval and preference-ranking variants improve CTR by 2.96% and conversion by 1.33%, respectively, over corresponding production baselines. Under matched training conditions, the training infrastructure increases supervised-target throughput by 84%. In serving, disaggregation reduces TransX's total serving cost by nearly 80% versus colocated serving at matched traffic and SLO, enabling approximately 5X the per-request model FLOPs of the HSTU-style production baseline at the same total serving cost. At matched per-request model FLOPs, TransX delivers approximately 4X higher request throughput per H200. These results show that online serving cost need not grow proportionally with recommendation model capacity.

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models reduce computation by routing each token to a small set of experts, but each selected expert is still used at a fixed precision. We present DPA-MoE, a runtime precision control framework that makes routed experts any-precision. DPA-MoE encodes expert feed-forward weights as prefix-decodable chunks using SharedScale AnyBCQ, profiles per-expert reconstruction residuals offline, and allocates chunks at inference time using live router gate scores. This post-routing allocation lets a single encoded MoE checkpoint operate at multiple effective weight budgets without retraining or re-encoding. Experiments on Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeekMoE-16B show that DPA-MoE improves KL divergence and downstream accuracy over uniform chunk allocation at matched budgets, with modest steady-state throughput overhead in our prototype.

Repeated Bertrand competition is a stringent benchmark for algorithmic pricing: in homogeneous-goods markets, standard learning procedures typically select the stage-game Nash outcome with prices near marginal cost. We show that LLM pricing agents can escape this benchmark, depending on deployment decisions. LLMs outperform standard online learners through faster adaptation, but this advantage largely disappears against full-information learners. An extracted surrogate algorithm illustrates how LLMs in specific deployments profit from regularly undercutting slower learners. Agents that post one price every period from raw market histories that include rival prices can sustain supracompetitive regimes in self-play. Agents that periodically emit probability distributions from summarized histories of their own profits instead move toward the competitive region in self-play. Markets mixing these interfaces can generate dynamic regimes in which LLMs establish a high-price anchor and regularly undercut it to capture demand. These regimes can keep both competitors' prices above marginal cost. Deployment choices shape these outcomes, shifting the focus from stationary equilibria to dynamic pricing regimes.
