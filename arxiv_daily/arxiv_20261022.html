Multimodal click-through rate (CTR) prediction is central to search systems, where ranking depends on item content, query relevance, and user preference. Existing approaches incorporate content through dense embeddings or coarse discrete IDs, yet effectively integrating fine-grained heterogeneous content with ID-based predictors remains challenging. We propose FineSID, a progressive learning framework that unifies content tokenization, query--item semantic pretraining, and personalized post-training via a shared fine-grained discrete representation. Each stage optimizes an explicit objective while preserving token-level access to content. First, a pretrained multi-scale visual tokenizer and text tokenizers convert heterogeneous content into fine-grained Semantic IDs (SIDs), explicitly aligning them with the ID features. Second, full-parameter semantic pretraining under query--item relevance supervision establishes a robust matching prior, jointly optimizing the interactions of multimodal SIDs and conventional fields within a shared predictor. Third, this initialized predictor is directly fine-tuned with user click supervision to capture implicit personalized preferences. Extensive offline and online evaluations demonstrate substantial gains in both standard CTR prediction and core business metrics. Furthermore, FineSID improves within-product creative discrimination, demonstrating its semantic generalization beyond conventional ID memorization.

In sponsored-shopping auctions, one advertiser may win several positions, and a product that is never displayed may determine the prices of several winners. Under generalized second-price (GSP) pricing without self-pricing, these interactions couple allocation and payment through a single global ranking. We prove that exact autobidding equilibria under budget and return-on-investment (ROI) constraints can be computed in polynomial time whenever either the number $n$ of advertisers or the number $K$ of display slots is fixed. Our algorithms rely on two structural reductions. With few advertisers, the payment polytopes of tied bid layers combine by a Minkowski sum, giving a fixed-dimensional representation of exponentially many rankings. With few slots, budget equilibria admit threshold recovery and an exact submodular description of attainable payments at the display boundary. For ROI, we prove that every group-consecutive equilibrium has a representative with at most $K+2$ nonunit multipliers, preserving the item and price in every displayed slot. In each case, the reduction leads to polynomially many systems of polynomial equations and inequalities in bounded dimension. The algorithms compute exact multipliers and explicit distributions over valid group-consecutive rankings, with support at most $n+1$; for ROI with few slots, this improves to $\min\{n+1,K+3\}$.

Existing sequential recommenders usually allocate a fixed computational budget to every item in a user's interaction history, regardless of the item's contribution to the final prediction. We challenge this de facto design with two diagnostic findings. (1) Non-uniform computational need: items within the same sequence vary drastically in how much computation they require. Most converge after shallow processing while a critical minority only stabilizes at full depth. (2) Suboptimality of fixed allocation: given oracle access to the target item, a non-uniform depth assignment consistently outranks the fixed one, indicating that existing uniform computation both wastes capacity on already-converged items and sacrifices overall recommendation quality. These observations motivate a shift from fixed-depth computation to item-level adaptive reasoning. To this end, we propose LoopRec. LoopRec recursively applies a single weight-shared block and introduces a lightweight gate at each recursion boundary that makes a binary exit-or-continue decision for every item independently. Items whose representations have sufficiently matured exit early, while those requiring deeper reasoning continue to subsequent recursions. This design converts the fixed global depth into a learned, per-item property without auxiliary depth labels, and is readily adaptable to diverse backbone architectures. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks with four backbone families demonstrate that LoopRec consistently outperforms matched fixed-depth counterparts. Further analysis reveals that LoopRec's learned allocation exhibits interpretable patterns aligned with our post-hoc oracle analysis: lower-frequency items and more recent interactions naturally receive deeper computation. Our code is available at https://github.com/Elpmis117/LoopRec.

查看完整摘要
Generative recommenders replace the retrieve-then-rank cascade with a single model: beam search over semantic item identifiers performs retrieval, and the same likelihood performs ranking. We show that this shared score makes them forget long-tail items twice. In retrieval, beam search keeps identifier prefixes by the probability mass of their subtrees, so tail items are pruned early, and a wider beam admits few more of them. In ranking, the likelihood places tail items below head items even when they are admitted, so expanding retrieval alone brings little or no gain. Both failures reflect missing item-level memorization (i.e., direct recall of which items co-occur), which collaborative filtering provides and shared-prefix generation lacks. To this end, we propose CAPE (Co-occurrence-Aware Pool Expansion), a training-free plug-in that restores memorization at both stages: (i) a memorization channel retrieves co-occurrence-supported tail items outside the beam and scores them with the frozen generator, and (ii) memorization-calibrated ranking fuses the generator likelihood with co-occurrence evidence while keeping the generator's first item. Experiments with two generative backbones on product and music domains show that CAPE significantly improves long-tail accuracy over the backbones and over re-ranking baselines that share its candidates, preserves or improves overall accuracy, and adds a small fraction of the latency.

Generative recommendation over discrete Semantic IDs has become a powerful paradigm for item retrieval. Real-world systems, however, frequently require real-time controllability to balance accuracy against objectives such as diversity. Existing autoregressive methods generate tokens strictly from left to right. This fixed direction locks in early decisions, preventing holistic planning and making control difficult. Consequently, direct interventions often break the semantic structure, producing non-existent items. To address this, we propose ReGen, a framework that shifts from sequential decoding to parallel iterative refinement. ReGen reformulates generation as a mask-filling task, predicting all tokens simultaneously rather than one by one. This global view allows us to apply control signals directly to tokens with high-level semantics without retraining the model. To ensure that decoded candidates map to real items, we introduce a topology-constrained tree that iteratively corrects low-confidence predictions while strictly enforcing valid codebook paths during beam-based inference. Experiments show that ReGen provides a favorable accuracy-diversity trade-off under moderate guidance while maintaining high validity of generated Semantic IDs. The source code is available at: https://github.com/zhang-haichao/ReGen.

Among existing embedding compression approaches, low-rank factorization provides a simple and mathematically principled solution by approximating the embedding matrix with a reduced-rank decomposition. However, its practical effectiveness is often limited, as the resulting linear bottleneck constrains representational capacity. To overcome these limitations, we propose an enhanced factorization scheme that increases expressive power by introducing learnable non-linear projections within the compressed embedding layer. Our method is inspired by the Kolmogorov Arnold Networks (KAN) paradigm, which replaces fixed linear transformations with learnable univariate functions. Despite promising results in other domains, applications of KANs in NLP remain scarce and have so far been largely limited to final or task specific layers, rather than core representational components such as embedding layers. We evaluate the proposed approach by integrating the compressed embedding layer into the Skip-gram framework and comparing it against a standard low-rank baseline under matched parameter budgets on the Word Analogy and Semantic Similarity benchmarks. We further introduce an effective initialization strategy from a pre-trained, uncompressed embedding layer. The behavior of the model is analyzed both immediately after initialization and after a brief post-initialization refinement phase, considering training with the standard Skip-gram objective as well as variants augmented with distillation from the original embedding layer. Overall, our results show that augmenting low-rank factorization with learnable non-linear projections effectively mitigates the limitations of linear bottlenecks, yielding more faithful compressed embeddings under fixed dimensionality and suggesting a promising direction for embedding layer compression beyond purely linear methods.

Large language models (LLMs) have shown great potential as general-purpose user simulators for interactive systems. Prior studies have demonstrated that training on high-quality data can substantially improve model reasoning and downstream task performance. However, the upper bound of user simulation is still constrained by the model's reasoning paradigm: native chain-of-thought often degenerates into shallow pattern matching and fails to adequately model the behavioral factors underlying real user decisions. In this paper, we propose OmniSimulator, a novel training framework designed to improve the predictive ability of small LLMs for realistic user simulation on short-video platform environments. Specifically, OmniSimulator organizes behavior-oriented reasoning through a structured schema with five dimensions, carefully selects data from real user-behavior scenarios, and introduces a teacher model to generate high-quality reasoning traces under the proposed schema, helping small models learn behavior-relevant reasoning patterns rather than relying on superficial behavioral correlations. Based on this process, we construct 9,881 high-quality training instances from three months of historical interaction trajectories of 200 real users, averaging about 50 distilled examples per user. Experimental results show that OmniSimulator improves the average score of Qwen3-8B by 34.33%, achieves performance competitive with Claude-Sonnet-4.5, and supports smaller-scale, self-hosted deployment. These results suggest that learning structured behavior-oriented reasoning patterns is a promising direction towards scalable and high-fidelity LLM-based user simulation.
