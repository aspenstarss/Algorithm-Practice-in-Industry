Fairness-aware recommender systems often encode one fairness objective during training or apply a fixed re-ranking rule after scoring. In deployment, however, the appropriate fairness action can vary across requests because the candidate pool, item metadata, exposure debt, user preference history, and utility budget all change at runtime. We propose FAIRGate, a model-agnostic delivery controller that places an agentic policy-selection layer between a base ranker and the final top-K list. The agent does not define fairness objectives from scratch. It chooses from a predefined policy library covering long-tail exposure, item-group exposure balance, concentration control, calibration protection, and abstention. A deterministic executor then applies the selected policy through constrained repair, and a validator accepts the result only when relevance, feasibility, metadata, calibration, and audit constraints are satisfied. The final language-model component receives only the verified audit trace and produces evidence-linked narration after the ranking decision is fixed. This design gives the agent runtime responsibility while keeping fairness enforcement measurable, bounded, and replayable.

Generative AI assistants are becoming an advertising medium, embedding sponsored content in their answers. This setting departs from classical position auctions in two ways: an ad's click-through rate depends on the passage that hosts it, and because users stop at their first click, earlier ads take clicks from later ones. This competition for clicks makes truthfulness delicate: a higher bid can reshape the allocation and put a competitor ahead of the bidder, reducing the bidder's clicks. Existing mechanisms either attain a constant fraction of the optimal welfare (advertisers' total click value) but are not known to be truthful, or are truthful but their welfare guarantee degrades as the number of passages grows. We achieve both even when the answer and its editorial order are fixed before bidding. Assuming each ad's click-through rate when reached is known and unaffected by omitting other ads, we give a computationally efficient mechanism that is truthful in expectation and attains at least half the optimal welfare under free reordering. Our key idea, click thinning, omits ads at random, showing earlier ads less often so that each ad's overall click probability no longer depends on the order of the ads. Welfare maximization then reduces to a tractable matching problem, and solving it exactly with VCG-style payments makes truthful bidding optimal in expectation. Characterizing the click probabilities that omission can achieve sharpens the approximation factor from $2$ to $2-(1-δ)^{K-1}$ when at most $K$ ads are shown and each click-through rate is at most $δ$. This factor is the best possible for any order-preserving rule, even one that ignores incentives and computation, so truthfulness and computational efficiency incur no additional worst-case loss.

Iterative combinatorial auctions are used in spectrum allocation, transportation, and industrial procurement, but rounds are costly. We introduce FastCombo, a deep reinforcement learning approach to sequential price discovery. Bayesian pricing has sharply reduced auction rounds even against subgradient benchmarks tuned with hindsight for each instance, but relies on a one-step objective and repeated online inference and optimization. We prove that greedily maximizing immediate clearing probability can require arbitrarily more rounds in expectation than an informative policy, even with the true valuation distribution known. FastCombo learns price policies offline, assigning credit to queries for the future decisions they enable and producing each online price update with a single neural-network forward pass. We formulate the objective of minimizing expected rounds to clearing in single-minded auctions as a partially observable Markov decision process and train a recurrent bidder-item graph policy over complete auction trajectories. In a one-item experiment, all five trained policies learn an informative opening, reducing expected rounds from 1.85 to 1.65 relative to the tested Bayesian one-step rule. Across four domains from the Combinatorial Auction Test Suite, FastCombo achieves competitive clearing while reducing measured online price-selection time per auction by a factor of roughly 300-900 relative to the standard Bayesian baseline. These results demonstrate the value of learning sequential price policies for both information acquisition and online computational efficiency.

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong reasoning capabilities, motivating their use as the backbone of foundation recommendation models (FRMs). Existing methods enhance recommendations through explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning under a Think-then-Answer paradigm. However, explicit CoT incurs substantial inference overhead by generating lengthy reasoning traces and relies on manually designed templates that struggle to capture diverse, dynamic user interests. We propose OneLatent, an efficient latent reasoning framework that compresses explicit reasoning traces into several learnable latent tokens, enabling Latent-Reason-then-Answer inference without generating verbose traces. OneLatent first introduces Multi-View Adaptive CoT (MV-ACoT), which creates diverse, high-quality teacher-generated supervision by exploring user interests from multiple perspectives and automatically adapting reasoning complexity to each instance. Building on pretrained FRMs, it then uses a three-stage latent-token alignment paradigm to progressively internalize CoT traces into learnable latent tokens. Finally, a multistage curriculum-based post-training strategy activates latent-token reasoning for downstream recommendation tasks. Experiments on an industrial-scale Kuaishou dataset and the public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark show that OneLatent consistently outperforms explicit CoT-based methods and traditional baselines. Compared with the Think and No-Think variants of FRMs, OneLatent improves SID@64 by 17.44% and 9.33%, respectively, while achieving over 17x higher online inference throughput. We further develop a production serving system for scalable, real-time FRM inference. An online A/B test in Kuaishou's local-services advertising scenario shows that deploying OneLatent with this system yields an estimated 9.6% revenue lift over strong online baselines, including OneRec and OneReason.

General-purpose embeddings trained on profile similarity often misrank candidates in professional networks, where relevance is inherently complementary rather than symmetric. For example, job seekers usually engage with recruiters rather than peers with similar credentials. Current user recommendation systems are often built on profile similarity or social signals such as shared connections, which are effective in friend-oriented social networks where users tend to connect with similar people or within existing social circles. However, this does not hold in professional platforms like LinkedIn, where interactions are often goal-driven and cross-role interactions, requiring matching based on complementary intents rather than similarity or social proximity. In this paper, we propose Preference-Aligned Intent Retrieval, a retrieval framework that is designed to model user intent preference instead of profile similarity. Our approach is inspired by the preference-learning perspective of Direct Preference Optimization, but instantiates preference learning as an exposure-conditioned multi-negative contrastive objective over embeddings. This formulation simplifies training and scales efficiently to large candidate sets without requiring an explicit reference model. Furthermore, we incorporate user interaction history by aggregating click histories with positional encoding to capture temporal order, which enables constant-cost updates of user representations over a fixed history window, allowing new interactions to be incorporated without re-encoding history. As a result, personalization can be updated efficiently using lightweight CPU operations without repeated LLM inference during serving. We evaluate PAIR on LinkedIn's Premium retrieval task. A 0.6B parameter model achieves a MAR@100 of 0.45.

Deep neural networks often struggle to provide reliable confidence estimates, making post-hoc calibration essential for reliable decision-making. Existing post-hoc methods largely rely on score-based transformations, which improve marginal calibration but often fail to capture spatially varying miscalibration in local regions of the learned feature space. To address this gap, we propose Local Adaptive Probability Estimation (LAPE), a post-hoc residual calibration framework. Specifically, LAPE treats the global calibrator output as a stable anchor and refines the individual prediction by leveraging non-parametric local evidence from neighboring calibration samples. As a plug-and-play refinement module, LAPE effectively utilizes the information about feature-space geometry and can reuse the calibration data that are used to construct the global calibrator. Furthermore, we extend LAPE to the setting with multi-view inputs through Cascade Feature Augmentation Fusion (CFAF). Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed methods can improve the calibration quality of various existing baselines and remain efficient and robust across different settings.

Agentic recommendation pursues shopper goals through sequences of context-dependent decisions. Developing and evaluating such policies requires faithful recommendation world models that capture both the interactive shopping environment and users' behavioral responses over long interactions. Existing resources rarely provide long-horizon, fine-grained real-user trajectories or support trajectory-level evaluation and offline validation against production A/B results. We introduce UserArena, a real-world e-commerce interactive benchmark containing 64,729 session trajectories from 11,010 users, spanning 566,102 items and 13 action types. UserArena pairs these trajectories with an interactive environment and supports offline validation against outcomes from a controlled production A/B test, enabling five-level evaluation from output validity and local decisions to sequential behavior, personalization, and outcomes. To improve simulator fidelity over these long-horizon interactions, we further propose On-Policy Trajectory Distillation (OPTD), which uses trajectory-level intent to provide dense teacher targets on student-generated rollouts. Experiments show that OPTD achieves the strongest overall fidelity among the evaluated simulators under UserArena's evaluation protocol and most closely reproduces the production policy effect in offline simulation.
