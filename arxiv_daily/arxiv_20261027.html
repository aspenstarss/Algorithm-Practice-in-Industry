Generative AI interfaces can insert sponsored content at multiple points in an answer, where click-through rates (CTRs) depend on the advertiser-position pair. Under the cascade model, users read ads sequentially and stop after their first click, so both ad assignment and display order affect welfare. For arbitrary pair-specific CTRs, Balseiro et al. (WWW 2026) gave a $4/(1-\varepsilon)$-approximation whose monotonicity has not been established, and a monotone $O(\log m)$-approximation for $m$ candidate positions. Earlier truthful constant-factor mechanisms require factorized CTR models and do not apply. Whether monotonicity is compatible with a constant-factor approximation in this general setting was left open by that work. We give a polynomial-time mechanism for arbitrary pair-specific CTRs and any limit on displayed ads that is truthful and individually rational in expectation and achieves a 2-approximation to optimal cascade welfare. Our mechanism selects an advertiser-position matching and a designated last ad, then randomly thins earlier ads so that each selected ad's expected cascade CTR is the same fraction of its standalone CTR. This yields an exact closed-form expression for expected welfare, which we maximize over a bid-independent lottery range using linear programming. Exact optimization ensures monotonicity, while a random-prefix decomposition proves the factor-two guarantee, which is tight for this range. We also show that exactly optimizing the previous welfare surrogate can violate monotonicity, and evaluate welfare and running time on synthetic instances.

Position auctions require assigning advertisers to positions and determining their display order. Under cascade behavior, earlier clicks prevent users from reaching later placements, coupling the click probabilities of selected advertisements. Existing methods achieve constant-factor welfare approximation and monotone logarithmic-factor approximation separately, leaving open whether both guarantees can be attained simultaneously. The difficulty is that welfare-preserving rounding need not maintain the monotonicity of individual click-through rates required for truthful bidding. We resolve this problem by developing a polynomial-time randomized mechanism that achieves a factor-6 welfare approximation and truthfulness in expectation for arbitrary public advertiser-position click probabilities. The mechanism optimizes a reach-weighted linear relaxation and converts its solution into feasible ordered allocations through matching decomposition, probability-bounded grouping, and calibrated attenuation. This construction compensates for cascade dependence and ensures that each advertiser's expected click-through rate equals exactly one sixth of its fractional click coordinate. The resulting identity transfers the monotonicity of the fractional allocation to the implemented mechanism, enabling exact incentive-compatible payments while preserving the welfare guarantee. The allocation lottery and payments are computable in polynomial bit time, with exact sampling in expected polynomial time.

Item-item similarity is one of the oldest and most foundational signals in recommender systems: two items are assumed to be similar when the same users consume them. Despite the sparsity and scale of interaction data, the implicit assumption across the field is that item co-occurrence reflects genuine shared preference, capturing what users like together. In this paper, we demonstrate that a substantial fraction of observed item-item similarity is an artifact of variance in user activity rather than shared taste. Even if every user consumed items completely independently, differing only in their total consumption volume, standard similarity measures still detect strong associations between items. We term this phenomenon phantom correlation: spurious item association that emerges strictly from heterogeneous user activity in the complete absence of preference structure. Session-based recommendation (SBR) infers user interest from limited interaction sequences, yet current models show a consistent performance drop when longer histories are available. To address this challenge, we introduce MIDA (Multi-Interest Decoding with Adaptive Aggregation for Session-based Recommendation), a lightweight and encoder-agnostic framework that provides a reformulation of the decoding stage.
