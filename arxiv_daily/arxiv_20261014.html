Conversational assistants are becoming the place where people ask what to buy, and sponsored content is moving into their answers. Several advertisers then compete for one sponsored slot, and all of them read the same conversation, so their willingness to pay usually moves together. Existing auctions for LLM advertising treat these values as independent. We therefore present AffMyerson, an auction that captures this correlation and turns it into revenue. We model the advertisers' values given the conversation as a multivariate normal and turn the economic premise of positive dependence (affiliation) into a sign condition on the precision matrix. We design a neural parameterization whose every output satisfies it. We prove that every prior in this family yields a truthful, ex-post individually rational auction, so estimation error can cost revenue but never truthfulness. We derive the conditional virtual values in closed form. By the design of the prior, pricing an auction needs no matrix inversion and takes O(n^2) time, fast enough to run at query time. We also work out the log-normal family used in empirical ad-auction work. There we prove that the auction is truthful for every prior and revenue-optimal when the prior is correct, and that the virtual value is regular everywhere whenever the conditional spread is below a loose threshold, which our fitted priors stay well under. With LLM-agent bidders, AffMyerson earns 6.2% more than a second-price auction with a tuned reserve, 6.1% more than Ronen's lookahead auction with the same prior, and 9.2% more than Myerson's auction run as if values were independent. This gain is still 4.5% on real keyword search queries where willingness to pay is hard to read. The correlation comes from common demand. Agents that judge the conversation bid together and agents told to calculate do not. The gain from conditioning moves with the correlation.

We propose a new framework for banner ad auctions, leveraging diffusion models to allow for multiple bidders to co-win a single banner ad opportunity, and in particular influence the ad shown on the banner depending on the strength of their bids, enabling a far richer outcome space compared to standard banner ad auctions, where there is a single winner who shows their ad. In our framework, the outcomes are images (what ad to show) and each bidder expresses their preferences over outcomes by declaring their value per unit of alignment between the image chosen by the mechanism and a textual description of their desired image. Our proposed mechanism combines the advertisers' bids and textual descriptions into a score that drives the reverse process of a diffusion model. This is used to sample a set of candidate images from which the welfare maximizing one is eventually chosen. Extensive experimentation evaluates our proposed mechanism around the following axes: (1) efficiency improvements of our allocation rule relative to the single-winner allocation rule; (2) monotonicity of our allocation rule; and (3) approximate incentive compatibility when combining our allocation rule with Myerson lemma-based or Clarke rule-based payments. Improvements along all these axes are shown with the number of Monte Carlo samples. Meanwhile, these benefits are attained while preserving the image quality.

Popularity bias is a well-known challenge in recommender systems, often leading to the over-exposure of popular items and reduced catalog coverage. In this work, we propose PR-entmax, a sparse prior-regularized objective that makes popularity correction context-dependent. We derive our method from a generalized variational principle in which an item popularity prior is incorporated directly into a sparse probability mapping, yielding a popularity-aware active support: the set of items receiving non-zero probabilities and hence non-zero gradients is determined jointly by contextual relevance and item popularity. As a result, the prior affects not only item probabilities, but also which items participate in gradient updates during training. The method requires no architectural changes and can be integrated into existing training pipelines by modifying only the loss function. At inference time, the prior term is removed, and items are ranked by the learned contextual relevance alone. Experiments across multiple benchmark datasets and sequential recommendation architectures show that PR-entmax consistently reduces popularity bias while preserving or improving recommendation relevance, advancing the relevance-debiasing trade-off.

The randomized primal-dual framework of Devanur, Jain, and Kleinberg (SODA 2013) splits each unit matching gain between endpoint dual variables according to the matched offline rank. The gain-sharing function affects the certificate but not Ranking's decisions. Keeping the splitting rule and expected edgewise feasibility criterion fixed, we define certifying capacity as the strongest uniform guarantee obtainable by functional optimization over a prescribed function class. Its shortfall from Ranking's actual competitiveness is the certifying gap. We study unweighted online bipartite matching under adversarial arrivals on graphs with online degree at most $d$ and on $d$-regular graphs. The optimized protocol is tight on both degree-one classes and in the unrestricted setting, but has a strictly positive gap on both degree-two classes, even over all measurable nonnegative complementary splits. In both degree-two settings, we determine Ranking's exact competitiveness and the exact certifying capacity over nondecreasing gain-sharing functions. For general $d$, we optimize over nondecreasing, right-continuous functions $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ with $f(1)=1$, using their probability-measure representation. Both the certifying capacity and actual competitiveness converge to $1-1/e$ in both instance families as $d\to\infty$. The resulting gap is at most $1/(2ed)+O(d^{-2})$ for online degree at most $d$; on $d$-regular graphs, it vanishes but is at least $(1/(2e)-o(1))/d$. These results distinguish function-class restrictions from limitations of the fixed protocol and motivate tighter randomized primal-dual frameworks.

Semantic-ID-based generative recommendation unifies retrieval and ranking through autoregressive decoding but differs structurally from discriminative models, which can directly access rich target-item side features. The attributes are compressed into a finite codebook during quantization, while the target item remains undetermined during decoding and its features cannot be looked up. This target-side feature vacuum weakens generation at each semantic-ID level through high conditional entropy and cascading errors, and final ranking through missing target-side feature crossing. Existing methods interact with candidate representations or generate discrete attribute tokens, but none reconstructs a continuous target-side representation inside decoding to support both stepwise generation and ranking. We propose TacGR (Stepwise Target-Side Feature Anticipation and Crossing for Generative Recommendation). Before decoding each codeword, TacGR anticipates a continuous target-side feature vector and crosses it with the semantic prefix and user behavior sequence inside the decoder. The crossed representation conditions current-level generation and is directly used for ranking. Continuous-vector regression risks a learnable target collapsing with its prediction and prediction noise propagating into generation through attention. TacGR addresses these challenges with a semantic-anchoring auxiliary tower and a unidirectional visibility mask, respectively. For extremely long industrial behavior histories, it introduces gated convolutional denoising to suppress noise and adapts the pyramid memory funnel to reduce inference cost. Offline experiments on a large-scale Lazada dataset show significant gains over OneRec, UniRec, and UniSGR. Online A/B testing improves IPV, Order, and GMV by 0.79%, 5.55%, and 3.09%, respectively.

In particular, numeric Document Identifiers (DocIDs) are generally more efficient because they are shorter and generally fixed-length sequences over a small vocabulary, requiring fewer decoding steps during beam search. These results show that an appropriate DocID design can heavily reduce retraining costs when adapting generative retrievers to new documents.

At a given prefix, multiple next-token candidates may be plausible, yet shape subsequent reasoning and differ in their chances of leading to the correct answer. Whether these differences are reflected in internal representations remains unclear.
